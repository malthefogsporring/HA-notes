\section{2-fold symmetric sequences}
Consider the category $\mathbf{E}^{disp}$. It is the free twofold symmetric monoidal category on one generator. As a consequence, there is an equivalence

$$LFun^{\lplus,\rplus}\big( Fun(\mathbf{E}^{disp},An),Fun(\mathbf{E}^{disp},An)\big)\simeq Fun(\mathbf{E}^{disp},An)$$
where $Fun(\mathbf{E}^{disp},An)$ has the twofold Day convolution product with respect to the Cartesian product. (Note the Day convolution swaps orientation.) There is a monoidal product on the left category given by composition of functors, which we can transfer to the right category. We call \textit{the opposite} of this product the (twofold) composition product $\circ$.

Let us unpack the definition of $\circ$. Let $O,U\in 2SymSeq(An):=Fun(\mathbf{E}^{disp},An)$. Under the equivalence $O$ corresponds to the functor $O':\colim よ_{G} \mapsto \colim O^{\oplus_G}$. We therefore have $$O\circ U=U'\circ O'(よ_{1}):O'(よ_{1})=O\simeq \colim よ_{G}\mapsto \colim U^{\oplus_G}$$

First let's unpack the colimit. It is the colimit of  $$\int_{\mathbf{E}^{disp}}O\xrightarrow[]{\pi} \mathbf{E}^{disp}\xrightarrow[]{よ}2SymSeq(An),$$ where $\int_{\mathbf{E}^{disp}}O$ is the category of elements; objects look like pairs $(G,x\in G)$. Since this is a disconnected category, we can split it into a coproduct and a connected colimit. However, this connected colimit is complicated -- it consists of $B\Sigma_n$'s and maps between them that are not necessarily equivariant. In other words, it is some complicated stratified space.

We would like to unpack this colimit more, separating out orbits of some $G-$action, and using the copower on $An$ to say $\colim_{x\in O(G)}Y\simeq O\times Y$. However, we are not sure how to do this yet.

Let us also unpack $U^{\oplus_G}$. This is the Day convolution, so it is the colimit
$$\colim_{\oplus_G H_i\rightarrow H}\prod_{i\in G} U(H_i).$$

\begin{question}
    What are monoids for this monoidal product?
\end{question}

\begin{question}
    Can we generalize this to symmetric sequences in a general closed symmetric monoidal $C$? Can we remove the closed hypothesis at the cost of turning the product normal oplax?
\end{question}

\end{question}
